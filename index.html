When a plumbing problem is an emergency

Some problems can wait for morning and some cannot. A pipe that is spraying, water coming through a ceiling, sewage backing up into a tub or a heater leaking onto the floor are emergencies, and our 24/7 line is for them. A slow drain or a dripping faucet is usually something we can schedule.

  • Water you cannot stop, or a main valve that will not close.
  • Sewage or dirty water coming up from a drain.
  • A burst or frozen-then-thawed pipe flooding a floor.
  • No water at all to a house or building.
  • A gas water heater with a smell of gas. Leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first.

If you are not sure, call and describe it. We will tell you whether it is an emergency visit, a same-day appointment when available or something that can wait.

What to do in the first five minutes

  1. Stop the water

    Close the main shutoff, often near the meter box in front of the house. For one fixture, close its stop valve.

  2. Cut the power near water

    Turn off breakers for wet rooms. Do not stand in water near outlets or appliances.

  3. Call the 24/7 line

    Tell us the street, what you see and what you have already shut off.

  4. Limit the damage

    Move rugs and electronics, soak up what you can and open a low drain if you safely can.

These steps matter more than speed. Most flooding is made worse by water that keeps running while people look for the plumber.

Not sure it is an emergency?

Call the 24/7 line and describe it. We will tell you plainly.
