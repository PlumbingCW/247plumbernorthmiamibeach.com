Water you cannot see is still an emergency

Leak detection in North Miami Beach starts with a symptom, not a pipe. The meter keeps moving with every tap closed, the water bill jumps, a ceiling stain spreads or a floor feels warm. Our 24/7 line takes these calls, and same-day appointments are offered when available. If water is actively coming through a ceiling or wall, shut off the main valve and switch off power to that area before you call.

Guessing wastes money. Opening a wall in the wrong place costs more than the leak detection that would have told you where to cut. We use a sequence of tests, from a simple meter check to listening gear and thermal imaging, so the repair starts at the right spot.

Meter still running or the bill went up

Start with the meter. Turn off every fixture, the ice maker and the irrigation, then watch the dial or the low-flow indicator. If it keeps moving, water is leaving the system somewhere.

Next we split the system. Closing the main valve at the house and checking whether the meter still moves tells us if the leak is between the meter and the house, which is usually the underground service line, or inside the house. That one test often saves hours, and it is how we sort out high bills with no visible leak.

NMB Water's 2025 report describes the water system serving the city and nearby towns, and your bill comes from the utility that serves your address. Ask that utility how to read your meter and what usage history it can share.

Ceiling, wall and attic leaks

A stain on a ceiling is where the water showed up, not where it started. Water travels along framing and pipes before it drips, so the real source can sit several feet away.

We check the stain with a moisture meter, trace the path with thermal imaging and test the lines above. Supply lines, drain lines and the air handler condensate drain each leak differently, and we tell them apart before cutting. If the source is a failed pipe, see burst pipe repair.

Leaks under the floor and under the slab

Many homes here sit on a concrete slab, and plumbing runs in or under it. A hot spot on the floor, a sudden bill spike or the sound of running water at night can mean a slab leak, and our slab leak repair page explains the repair choices.

If your home has a crawl space or a raised floor, we check the pipes beneath it for damp insulation, mold smell and active drips. Those spaces make a leak easier to reach, but also easier to ignore.

Yard, irrigation, meter box and pool lines

Not every leak is in the house. A soggy patch of lawn can be the water service line, a failed irrigation valve or a cracked pool or spa supply line. A wet meter box is often a fitting problem close to the street.

We test the underground water service line separately from the irrigation system and the pool plumbing, so you know which one needs work. For the line between the meter and the house, see water service line repair.

Protecting your claim and your records

Repairs that follow detection are covered by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is built on the IPC. Keep the meter readings from your test, photos of damage and our written findings. If you file an insurance claim, these show when you noticed the problem and what caused it. Policies differ, so read yours or ask your insurer what is covered.

Commercial buildings

A leak in a store, an office or a multi-unit building has to be found without shutting down the whole property. We isolate zones, test risers and check shared lines, and we write up findings for the manager or owner.

Our commercial plumbing page covers after-hours dispatch and scheduling around business hours.

What affects the price of leak detection

Leak detection is a diagnostic, and the price depends on how many tests the problem needs.

Size of the property
A condo unit is a smaller job than a large house or a building.
Where the leak could be

Number of tests
A meter test is quick. Acoustic and thermal work takes more time.
Access
Furniture, cabinets and tight spaces slow the work.
Repair and restoration
