A water heater installation you plan instead of rush

Water heater installation in North Miami Beach is the planned version of replacement. You are upgrading, adding a unit, finishing a renovation or moving to a different fuel, and you have time to decide. We help you choose the type and size, check the space and utilities, and schedule the work.

This page is for non-urgent installs. If your tank is leaking now, go to emergency water heater replacement instead.

What we check before we quote

  • The space: height, clearance, floor condition and drain access.
  • Fuel and supply: gas line size, electrical capacity or both.
  • Venting and combustion air for gas units.
  • Water pressure and the shutoff valves already in place.
  • Building rules if the home is a condo or in an association.

New builds and renovations

In new construction or a kitchen and bath remodel, the heater location is part of the plumbing layout. We coordinate with the rest of the job and the trades involved, which is why our plumbing installation page lists the related work.

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of North Miami Beach building department about the permit and inspection steps that apply to your address.

Moving from gas to electric

Some homeowners and condo owners switch fuels when a gas line is unavailable or when they want a heat pump unit. The change affects the electrical panel, the wiring, the breaker and sometimes the room.

We check panel capacity first. If it cannot support the load, we tell you what the upgrade involves before you commit to a heater.

Heat pump water heaters in a warm climate

A heat pump heater pulls heat from the air around it, so it works best in a space with enough volume and some warmth. It also cools and dehumidifies that space slightly, which can be a plus in a Florida garage.

It needs a condensate drain and enough clearance for airflow. A tiny closet usually does not work, and we say so when we visit.

Safety items we set up on every install

  • A temperature and pressure relief valve with a proper discharge line.
  • A drain pan with a drain route where the location calls for one.
  • Shutoff valves you can reach.
  • Correct venting and combustion air for gas units.
  • An expansion tank where the system needs one.

Code requirements for a new heater

A new install must meet the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the IPC. For gas units, venting and combustion air matter. For electric units, circuit size matters. Permit rules depend on the job and the municipality, so ask the building department that covers your address what applies.

Setting it up so it lasts

A heater lasts longer when the installer sets the pan, the drain, the expansion control and the temperature correctly. We also show you where the shutoffs, the relief valve and the drain valve are so you can act in an emergency.

NMB Water recommends flushing a heater at least twice a year if water is discolored, and we leave maintenance notes with the install.
