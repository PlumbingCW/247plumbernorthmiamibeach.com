No hot water or a leaking tank

A water heater that quits or leaks is an emergency, because the tank holds dozens of gallons and a leak can reach the floor fast. Our water heater repair plumbers answer the line 24/7 in North Miami Beach and offer same-day appointments when available. If you see water around the tank, shut the cold water supply valve on top of the heater and turn the power or gas to the unit off before you call.

No hot water is the other common call, and it is often a failed element, thermostat, igniter or pilot assembly rather than a dead tank. We test before we recommend anything, because a repair that costs a fraction of a new unit is worth knowing about.

What to do while you wait

  • Close the cold water shutoff on the pipe above the heater.
  • Switch an electric unit off at the breaker. On a gas unit, turn the gas control to off or pilot.
  • Keep towels or a wet vac ready, and move anything stored near the tank.
  • Do not open the drain valve unless a plumber tells you to. The water can be scalding.
  • Photograph any leak and the data label on the tank. It helps us bring the right parts.

A leak under the tank: repair or replace

Where the water comes from decides the answer. A leak at a fitting, a drain valve or a temperature and pressure relief valve can be repaired. Water seeping from the tank body means the steel liner has failed, and no repair fixes that.

Callers often find water at the edge of a garage slab and wonder if it is the heater. We check the fittings, the pan and the drain line first, then decide. If the tank itself is leaking, see water heater replacement for what happens next.

Hybrid and heat pump units add a compressor and fan to the electric heater, so a no-hot-water call there may be a control or a condensate issue. We service them too.

How we test before we recommend

A meter tells us if power is reaching the elements and if the thermostats and elements read correctly. On gas units we check the pilot, thermocouple, venting and gas valve. We also look at sediment, the anode condition if accessible and the age on the label.

NMB Water's 2025 report suggests flushing a water heater and household plumbing at least twice a year if water is discolored, and sediment is often what we find in a neglected tank. A flush may help an older heater but will not save one with a failed liner.

What the manufacturer and the code expect

Every tank heater needs a temperature and pressure relief valve piped to a safe termination. The IPC calls for this and the manufacturer's instructions repeat it. We check the valve and the discharge pipe whenever the heater is open, because a missing or blocked relief line is a safety problem.

Repair or replace: how we decide

We weigh the age of the heater, the cost of the part, whether the tank is leaking and whether the unit still meets your household's needs. A newer heater with a bad element is a repair. An old tank with a seeping bottom is not.

If you are considering a change, we also explain options such as a tankless unit, a heat pump model or moving a gas unit to electric. Details are on our tankless water heater page.
