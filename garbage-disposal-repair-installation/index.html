A disposal emergency: jammed, leaking or dead

A jammed or leaking garbage disposal makes the kitchen unusable. Our garbage disposal repair plumbers in North Miami Beach answer the line 24/7 and offer same-day appointments when available. Turn the disposal off at the switch, never put a hand inside and unplug it or turn off the breaker before anyone works on it.

Humming without grinding means the blades are stuck. A silent unit may have a tripped reset button or a dead motor, and a leak underneath points to a seal, a mounting ring or a drain connection.

Safe checks before we arrive

  • Switch the disposal off and unplug it or turn off the breaker.
  • Never reach into the disposal. Use tongs for an object you can see.
  • Press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit.
  • Run cold water and check under the sink for leaks.

Jams, hums and leaks

Most jams can be cleared with a hex key turned at the bottom of the unit, but a unit that hums and trips repeatedly may have a worn motor or a damaged flywheel.

A leak from the side is usually a seal or a hose connection. A leak from the bottom is often the unit itself, and replacement is the answer. If the sink also drains slowly, see drain cleaning.

Disposals and dishwashers share a drain

The dishwasher drain hose connects to a port on the disposal or to the sink drain. If that port still has its knockout plug in place after a new install, the dishwasher cannot drain and backs up into the sink.

We check the connection, the high loop or air gap on the hose and the trap arm slope. These small details cause most of the dishwasher and disposal complaints that look like a clog.

What not to put down a disposal

  • Grease, oil and fat, which coat the drain line.
  • Coffee grounds and eggshells in large amounts.
  • Fibrous scraps such as celery, corn husks and artichokes.
  • Pasta, rice and bread, which swell in water.
  • Bones, fruit pits and anything hard.

A disposal is not a trash can. The cleaner your line stays, the fewer calls you make.

Power and switch problems

If the unit is silent and the reset button does nothing, the cause may be the switch, the outlet or the wiring. We test power at the unit and check for a tripped circuit.

Hardwired units and plug-in units need different installs, and local rules on wiring vary, so ask the City of North Miami Beach building department if you plan a new circuit.

Choosing a unit for your kitchen

Disposals vary in motor size, grind stages and sound insulation. A household that cooks daily benefits from a stronger motor, and a light-use kitchen is fine with a standard one.

Space under the sink matters too. A deep unit may not fit above a trap and a filter, and we check the clearance before we buy or install.

Wiring and drain rules for disposals

A disposal needs a switch or plug the user can reach and a drain that is vented and sloped. The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the IPC, covers the trap and drain. Electrical connections fall under the electrical code, so we tell you if a licensed electrician is needed. Check your condo declaration for limits on disposals.

Installing a new disposal

We select a unit sized for your household, connect the drain, the dishwasher hose and the power, and test for leaks. A tight under-sink space and an older drain layout affect the job, so we check before quoting.
