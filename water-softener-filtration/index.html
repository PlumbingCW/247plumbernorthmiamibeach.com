Test your water before you buy a system

Water softener and filtration service in North Miami Beach starts with a question: what is in your water? NMB Water's 2025 report describes how the city treats its groundwater, which draws on the Biscayne and Floridan aquifers and uses lime softening, nanofiltration or reverse osmosis, but it does not publish a hardness figure. So we test the tap in your home.

If the test shows scale, taste or odor problems, we recommend a system. If it does not, we say so.

What your water looks like

The 2025 report says the system draws groundwater from the Biscayne aquifer, which sits roughly 10 to 200 feet down in porous limestone, and from the deeper Floridan aquifer, which is brackish and treated by reverse osmosis. The water is blended and disinfected before delivery, and the report lists chloramine as the disinfectant.

Hardness in your home can still vary by address and by plumbing. Spotting on glass, scale on fixtures and a crust on a showerhead are signs worth testing. Ask NMB Water for its annual report and test your tap with a kit or a lab.

Service and repair of an existing system

Many calls are not new installs. A softener may not regenerate, the brine tank may be bridged with salt or a valve may stick. We check the head, the drain line, the injector and the brine system, and we say when a part is not worth replacing.

Scale also affects other equipment. If a tankless heater shows lowered output, see tankless water heater service.

Salt, drains and where the discharge goes

A salt-based softener regenerates by flushing brine to a drain, and it needs a proper air gap and a drain location that is allowed. Not every garage or closet has one.

We check the drain, the electrical outlet and the bypass valve before choosing a model, and we plan the layout so you can reach the brine tank for salt.

A simple care schedule

  • Check the salt level every month or two.
  • Break up any salt bridge in the brine tank.
  • Replace sediment and carbon cartridges on the maker's schedule.
  • Test the water once a year to see if the system still matches the need.
  • Keep the manual and the model number.

Condos, rentals and what you can change

Condo owners and renters have limits on whole house treatment. Many buildings control the main line, so a whole house softener may not be allowed, while a point-of-use filter at the kitchen tap usually is.

Tell us what you own and what the association allows, and we recommend something that fits.

Know your water first

Treatment should match what is in your water, so test before you buy. NMB Water's 2025 report does not publish a hardness figure. Ask the utility or test your tap. Softeners need a drain connection, and the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the IPC, sets requirements for backflow protection on treatment equipment.

Lead and household plumbing

NMB Water reports no lead service lines in the city system, and it also notes that lead can come from household plumbing and fixtures. If you want to check, ask for a lead test from a certified lab, and ask us about older solder and fixtures. A filter certified for lead reduction is one option, and the manufacturer's instructions for it matter.
