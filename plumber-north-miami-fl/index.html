Housing built in the postwar boom

North Miami grew fast after World War II. The city was incorporated in 1926 and grew steadily through the postwar decades. That history means many blocks hold houses of similar age, with plumbing of similar age that is now being asked to last longer than anyone planned.

For you, that usually shows up as slow main lines, aging supply pipe and water heaters installed by the previous owner. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock from our North Miami Beach base, and a plumber arrives knowing which problems this housing mix tends to produce. See drain cleaning for slow lines and repiping for pipe that keeps leaking.

Drain and sewer lines under older streets

A house built in the 1950s or 1960s may have a cast iron or clay line that has shifted or scaled. Roots work into joints, and a line that sags holds water and waste, so the clog returns every few months.

We start with a camera when the history looks like that. A sewer camera inspection shows the actual pipe, and a line with roots and a sag leads to sewer line repair rather than another cable run.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak in North Miami

Water moves fast through a slab home. These steps limit the damage while the plumber is on the way.

  • Find the main shutoff valve, usually near the meter box in the front, and turn it off.
  • Switch off power at the breaker for any room with water on the floor.
  • Clear rugs, boxes and electronics from the wet area and open low drains if you can.
  • Call the 24/7 line and tell us the street, the room and where the water is coming from.

If the main valve will not turn, say so on the call. That changes what we bring.

Water in North Miami comes from the city's own utility

North Miami operates its own water utility and publishes its own annual Consumer Confidence Report. That is different from our home city, where NMB Water supplies the water. If a plumber tells you something about your water, ask which utility serves your address and read that utility's report.

If you notice discolored water, a metallic taste or lukewarm showers, a water heater flush may help. Our water heater repair page explains what to check first.

What to ask before you hire anyone

Any plumber you call should give a clear price before work starts and be licensed and insured. You can check a contractor's license on the state's Verify a Licensee page at myfloridalicense.com, and it takes about two minutes.

For an older North Miami house, also ask whether the quote covers the cause or only the symptom. A cable run clears a clog today, while a camera shows why it keeps returning. We explain both options and let you choose.

Plumbing work in North Miami follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and the city handles its own permits.
