A leak under your floor is an urgent problem

A slab leak is a water line leaking in or beneath the concrete floor of the house. In North Miami Beach, where so many homes are built on slabs, it shows up as a warm spot on the floor, the sound of water at night, a meter that keeps moving or water at the edge of a wall.

Our 24/7 line takes these calls and offers same-day appointments when available. Shut off the main water valve if water is reaching the floor, and keep the area clear.

The first job is to confirm it is a slab leak and not a surface or drain leak. Then we locate it so the repair is as small as the problem allows.

Signs of a slab leak

  • A warm or hot patch on the floor, even with the heat off.
  • The sound of running water when every fixture is off.
  • A water meter that moves with the house shut down.
  • Wet carpet, lifted flooring or cracks that were not there before.
  • A sudden jump in the water bill with no change in use.

How we locate it

We begin with a pressure test to confirm a supply line is leaking, then listen along the slab with acoustic gear. Thermal imaging picks up the heat pattern of a hot water line, and a pipe locator maps where the lines run.

The goal is a mark on the floor within a short distance of the leak. That keeps the opening small and the repair quote honest. See leak detection for the full set of tests.

Repair options for a slab leak

OptionHow it worksTrade-off
Spot repair through the slabCut the concrete, expose the pipe, fix the leakOpens the floor, then needs patching and finish work.
Reroute overheadInstall a new line in the wall or ceiling and abandon the old oneFewer concrete cuts but more drywall work.
Repipe the affected linesReplace the failing pipe with newHigher cost but fewer repeat leaks.
Epoxy or liningCoat or sleeve the pipe from the insideDepends on pipe type and condition.

If more than one leak shows up, the pipe may be failing along its length, and repiping is worth discussing.

Why slabs leak

Pipes can be rubbed through by concrete movement, corroded by soil and water chemistry or damaged by a poor joint. A leak is not always a sign of a bad installation, and it is not always a sign of aging either.

We look at the pipe material and the condition of the leaking section, because that tells us if one repair will hold. We also mention how your meter and bill behave, which are the early signals.

What to do before we arrive for a slab leak

  • Close the main valve if you hear water running or see damage.
  • Move rugs and furniture from damp or warm floors.
  • Switch off the water heater if a hot line may be leaking.
  • Write down the meter reading and the time.

Permits and records for slab work

Breaking a slab and replacing pipe may need a permit. The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is built on the IPC, covers the pipe materials and the testing of new lines. Permit rules depend on the job and the municipality, so ask the building department that covers your address what applies.

Insurance and documentation

If water has reached flooring or drywall, document it with photos and the date you noticed it. Many policies cover sudden damage but not the pipe, and rules differ, so call your insurer early.

We give you a written explanation of where the leak is and what it needs, which helps with a claim.
