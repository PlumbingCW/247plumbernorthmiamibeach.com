Emergency jetting for lines that keep backing up

Hydro jetting sends high-pressure water through a drain line with a nozzle that cleans the full circle of the pipe, not just a hole through the middle. We run it for North Miami Beach homes and businesses with backups that keep returning.

The 24/7 line takes the call when a main line stops at night or on a weekend. Same-day jetting is offered when the schedule has room, and until then stop using water and keep the cleanout area clear.

A cable punches a channel through a clog and leaves the grease film behind. The film grows back, and the homeowner calls again a month later. Jetting removes the film, the scale and most of the root hair, which is why it is the usual next step after a cable has failed.

When the snake got stuck or the clog came back

Callers tend to describe the same pattern. Someone ran a cable, it cleared for a while and then the line slowed again, or the cable stopped partway and would not go farther. Both mean the blockage is bigger than a cable can handle.

A stuck cable can also mean a bend, an offset joint or a crushed section of pipe. That is why we look at the line with a camera when the history suggests damage, because high pressure belongs inside a sound pipe. See sewer camera inspection for how that works.

When jetting is the wrong choice

Jetting is not a cure for every blockage. A pipe that is cracked, collapsed or separated at a joint will not hold up to the water, and pushing pressure through it can wash soil into the line or damage the pipe further.

  • A camera shows a broken or collapsed section, so the line needs sewer line repair first.
  • The pipe is very old and thin-walled and the camera shows heavy flaking.
  • The blockage is a solid object that must be pulled, not washed out.

We would rather tell you that on the phone than jet a line that cannot take it.

The right nozzle matters. A forward-facing head opens a tight clog, and a rear-facing head scours the pipe wall as it is pulled back.

Restaurants, shops and larger buildings

Commercial kitchens along the NE 163rd Street corridor and the Biscayne Boulevard side of the city push a lot of grease into their lines, and a cable alone rarely keeps up. Jetting on a regular schedule keeps a kitchen line open between pump-outs of the grease trap.

If you run a business or manage a building, the same method applies to long horizontal lines and floor drains. Our commercial plumbing page covers after-hours calls and how we schedule around opening times.

Rules and limits around jetting

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing is the statewide plumbing code. Its 2023 edition is built on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Florida amendments. Jetting does not usually need a permit on its own, but a repair found during the job might. Permit rules depend on the job and the municipality, so ask the building department that covers your address what applies.

A jetting visit step by step

  1. Camera first when needed

    If the history suggests damage we inspect the line before any pressure goes in.

  2. Access the line

    The jetter connects at a cleanout. Without one, a toilet may need to be pulled.

  3. Open then scour

    The nozzle opens the blockage, then cleans the pipe on the way back out.

  4. Confirm and record

    We run water, check every fixture and tell you what came out of the line.
