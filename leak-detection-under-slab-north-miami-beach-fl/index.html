Leak Detection Under a Slab in North Miami Beach, FL

A leak under concrete can run for months without a stain. Here are the signs to watch for and the way a plumber locates it before cutting.

Call (305) 850-6382

By the North Miami Beach 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 12, 2026

Signs of a leak under the floor

Water lines in a slab are hidden, so the signs show up indirectly. Floors may feel warm, which points to a hot water line. A hissing or running sound may be heard with everything off. A bill may jump, or a meter may spin when no one is using water.

Other signs are cracks in tile, damp carpet, a musty smell and low water pressure. None of them proves a slab leak alone. Together with a meter test, they make a strong case.

How we find it

  1. Confirm the leak

    We run a meter test and a pressure test to prove water is escaping.

  2. Separate hot from cold

    We isolate each system to find which line is leaking.

  3. Listen and scan

    Acoustic sensors and a thermal camera narrow the zone.

  4. Mark the spot

    We confirm with a second method and mark the floor.

What to do while you wait

  • Turn off the main water valve if you hear water running or see damage.
  • Move rugs, boxes and furniture away from damp areas.
  • Switch off the water heater if the leak is on the hot line.
  • Do not drill or break the floor yourself.
  • Take photos for your records.

If the floor is wet, call right away. See slab leak repair for the repair options once the spot is known.

What happens after we find it

We explain the options and the price before work starts. A single leak in good pipe may be a spot repair. A line with several leaks may be better rerouted or repiped.

You decide, and we do not start until you agree. We also tell you what the floor will look like when we leave.

Why we do not guess

Cutting a floor on a hunch can miss the leak by several feet and add repairs. Water under a slab can travel along the pipe or through the soil before it surfaces, so the wet spot is often not the leak.

Locating the leak first keeps the opening small and often saves the cost of a second hole. It also lets us tell you whether a reroute would make more sense than breaking concrete.

Pipe materials under slabs

Homes of different ages use different supply pipe. Copper is common and can develop pinholes. Older homes may have galvanized steel, which rusts from inside. Newer homes may use plastic pipe, which fails differently.

Ask us what your house has. If the pipe is old and has leaked once, ask about the condition of the rest. See repiping for when replacing the whole system is sensible.

After the leak is fixed

Once the repair is done, we pressure test the line again to prove it holds. The meter should stop moving when everything is off. If the floor was opened, the patch should be level and sealed before flooring goes back.

Keep the records of the detection and the repair. They help if you file an insurance claim or sell the house. Mention the repair when you list the property.

Why a second leak may appear

In a line that is old, fixing one pinhole can raise pressure at another weak spot. This is not a sign of bad work. It means the pipe is near the end of its life.

If you have had more than one leak, ask for a plan for the rest of the system. A planned repipe is much easier on a household than a series of emergencies.

Tools used for slab leaks

  • Pressure gauges to prove the system loses pressure.
  • Ground microphones to hear the leak through concrete.
  • Thermal cameras to show warm paths from hot lines.
  • Tracer gas in some cases, where sound alone is not enough.
  • Pipe locators to trace the route of the line.

Each tool has limits, which is why we use more than one.

A local note for North Miami Beach homes

Many houses in the area were built on slabs, and the water lines run through or under them. Pipe materials differ by decade, so the age of the house matters. NMB Water says it found no lead service lines in the city system in 2025, but leaks in household pipe are a separate matter.

If you live in a condo, a slab leak may be a shared issue, so tell management before work. Our two nearest service areas are North Miami and Sunny Isles Beach.

Repairs to pipe under a slab fall under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), including testing of the new line.
