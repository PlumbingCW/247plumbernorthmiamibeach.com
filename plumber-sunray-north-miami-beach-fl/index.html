A postwar neighborhood of single-family homes

Sunray is named on city planning maps as two parts, Sunray West in the north-central part of the city and Sunray East next to Greynolds Park and West Dixie Highway. A local planning summary describes Sunray West as almost entirely single-family homes, and Sunray East as similar with more mixed land use.

Miami-Dade County planning history lists Sun Ray Homes among the GI housing subdivisions built in 1946 to 1949. Greynolds Park, next door, opened in 1936 and was built by the Civilian Conservation Corps. Real estate listings put typical homes at three or four bedrooms and roughly 1,000 to 1,800 square feet, and show streets such as NE 172nd through NE 183rd Street and NE 13th through NE 21st Avenue.

Old homes are a reason to look, not proof of a problem

A house from the late 1940s is now close to 80 years old. That does not mean its pipes are bad. Many have been repaired, repiped or replaced over the decades, and we do not know which ones from the street. What age does mean is that nobody should guess.

If a drain keeps backing up, a sewer camera inspection shows what the line really looks like. If it finds a break, root intrusion or a sag, the next step is sewer line repair. If the pipes look sound, you save money by not replacing them.

Common jobs in Sunray

Single-family homes on small lots tend to call for the same handful of jobs.

  • Slow kitchen and bathroom drains, with drain cleaning as the usual first step.
  • Water heaters that leak, run out of hot water or have reached the end of their life. See water heater replacement.
  • Running toilets and worn faucet parts on the faucet repair page.
  • Questions about whole-house repiping after repeated leaks, which is a decision to make with the pipe in view.

Many Sunray homes also have yards with trees. Roots can find their way into older drain joints, which is another reason a camera is a better first step than repeated snaking.

Before we arrive: if you have a leak in your Sunray home

These steps help protect floors and walls while a plumber is on the way.

  • Close the fixture shutoff or the main water valve at the meter.
  • Switch off power at the breaker for any wet room.
  • Move rugs and furniture, and set out towels or buckets.
  • Call and tell us the street, the room and what you see.

If the main valve will not close, tell us on the call.

Near Greynolds Park and the Royal Glades Canal

Sunray sits close to Greynolds Park and the Royal Glades Canal, which means rain can leave standing water in some low yards. Do not assume that a wet spot near the house is a pipe leak. Try shutting off the water at the main and watching the meter. If the dial still moves, call.

Your city water bill will show which utility serves your address. NMB Water publishes an annual Consumer Confidence Report, and that report says brown or yellow water can point to rust or aging pipe.

Repairs and replacements here follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and we say which jobs need a permit when we quote.
