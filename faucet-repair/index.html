A valve that will not shut off cannot wait

A faucet or supply valve that will not shut off can flood a cabinet or a bathroom. Our faucet repair plumbers in North Miami Beach answer the line 24/7 and offer same-day appointments when available. Close the shutoff under the sink or at the main valve, put a bucket under any drip and call.

Most faucet calls are a worn cartridge, washer or O-ring. The difficult ones are valves that will not close, shower valves behind a wall and supply stops that break when you turn them.

Sink faucets

We repair or replace kitchen and bathroom faucets, including cartridge, ceramic disc and washer designs. If you have a particular brand, send us the name and we check parts before the visit.

A faucet that drips is wasting water you pay for, and a leak under the sink can soak the cabinet floor. See leak detection if the bill keeps rising.

Tub and shower valves

A shower that drips or a tub faucet that will not stop usually has a worn cartridge inside the wall. In many homes the valve can be repaired through the trim opening, and in others it needs access from behind.

A slow tub drain is a separate issue. See drain cleaning.

Supply stops and angle valves

The small valves under a sink or behind a toilet are meant to be turned off during repairs, but old ones often fail. A stop that leaks when you turn it, or does not close, needs replacing, and we do it before the main fixture work so the job is safe.

If water pressure at the faucet is low, see water service line repair for what else can cause it.

Kitchen faucets, pull-downs and sprayers

Kitchen faucets work harder than bathroom ones. A pull-down hose that drips under the sink, a side sprayer that loses pressure or a handle that sticks all trace back to a specific part we can replace.

We check the supply lines too. Braided lines age, and a burst line under a sink is a common source of cabinet flooding. If we see a bulge or rust on the fittings we tell you before it fails.

Condos, rentals and older bathrooms

Many fixtures in older condos and rentals have stops that have not been turned in years. They seize, leak when touched and sometimes snap. We replace them with quarter-turn valves so the next repair does not start with a gamble.

If you manage a rental, give us the unit number and an access contact, and we document the repair for your records.

Know your shutoffs

Every house has three levels of shutoff: the fixture stop under the sink, the room or branch valve if one exists and the main valve where water enters the house. Learn where each is and turn them once a year so they do not freeze in place.

A working shutoff turns a flooded cabinet into a ten-second fix. If yours do not turn, replacing them is cheaper than the flood.

What to do before we arrive for a faucet leak

  • Close the stop valves under the sink.
  • Place a bowl under the drip and dry the cabinet floor.
  • Take a photo of the faucet and any model name.

Lead and fixture age

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, built on the IPC, sets rules for fixtures in new work. NMB Water's 2025 report says lead can come from household plumbing and fixtures, even though the city found no lead service lines in its own system. If your faucets are old, replacing them is one way to reduce that source. Ask what the label on a new faucet says about its materials.

Replacing a faucet

We install faucets you supply or we provide, check the connections and test for leaks. Condo owners should check their association rules before replacing a shower valve.
