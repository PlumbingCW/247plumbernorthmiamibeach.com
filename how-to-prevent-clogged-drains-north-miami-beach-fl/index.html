How to Prevent Clogged Drains in North Miami Beach

Most clogs build slowly from things we send down every day. These habits keep them from forming, and the signs tell you when to call.

Call (305) 850-6382

By the North Miami Beach 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 12, 2026

Why drains clog in the first place

A drain clogs when something sticks to the pipe wall and catches the next thing, and then the next. Grease coats the inside of a kitchen line. Hair and soap form a mat in a shower trap. Wipes and paper towels do not break down and pile up at bends. Over weeks the opening narrows until water slows and stops.

Prevention is about stopping the first layer. The habits below cost little and take seconds, and they cut down on the calls that start with a gurgle and end with water on the floor.

Kitchen: grease, food and the disposal

  • Wipe pans with a paper towel before washing. Put the towel in the trash.
  • Pour cooled grease into a can, not down the sink.
  • Run cold water while the disposal works and for a short time afterward.
  • Keep coffee grounds, eggshells, rice and pasta out of the disposal.
  • Use a sink strainer to catch scraps.

Cold water helps because grease stays solid and moves along the pipe rather than melting and coating it further down. If your kitchen sink drains slowly, see drain cleaning.

Bathroom: hair, soap and what not to flush

Fit a hair catcher in every shower and tub drain, and clean it weekly. Hair is the main cause of slow tubs.

Only toilet paper and human waste belong in a toilet. Wipes labelled flushable, cotton swabs, floss and feminine products do not break down the way paper does. They collect on roots and rough spots in the pipe and build a clog that no plunger can reach. If that happens, see toilet repair.

When it is not a simple clog

If several drains slow at once, water gurgles in one fixture when another runs, or you smell sewage, the problem is in the main line. That is not a job for a store-bought liquid. Chemical openers can damage pipe and are dangerous to the plumber who has to open the line afterward.

In that case stop using water and call. A camera shows what is wrong. See sewer camera inspection.

A monthly drain routine that takes five minutes

Once a month, remove the stopper or strainer in each sink and tub and pull out what has collected. Rinse each drain with hot tap water for a minute. In the kitchen, run hot water after washing greasy dishes with a squirt of dish soap to carry film along the pipe.

Check the cleanout cap outside if you know where it is, and keep the area clear of plants and soil. A clear cleanout saves time when a plumber needs access.

Signs a drain is warning you

  • Water drains more slowly than it did a month ago.
  • You hear gurgling when another fixture runs.
  • A faint sewer smell returns after you run the water.
  • The toilet needs two flushes to clear.

Any one of these means the pipe is narrowing. Acting early with a cable is cheaper than waiting for a full stop. See drain cleaning for what the visit includes.

What not to put in a drain

  • Cooking grease, oil and fat.
  • Wipes of any kind, including those labelled flushable.
  • Coffee grounds and eggshells.
  • Hair, floss and cotton products.
  • Paint, concrete and plaster dust.

Paint and plaster are a special case. They harden in the pipe and can block it permanently. Wash tools in a bucket and dispose of the water properly.

Multi-unit and condo notes

In a building with a shared drain stack, what you put down your sink can affect your neighbors. Grease from one unit can coat the stack and cause a backup that floods the lowest unit first.

Follow the same habits as a house, and tell management if you smell sewage or hear gurgling. A building-wide cleaning is often more effective than unit-by-unit calls.

A local note for North Miami Beach homes

The older parts of the area have homes with decades-old drain lines. If you live in one, a slow drain may be the pipe rather than the habits, and a camera can tell the difference. Tree roots and sagging sections are common causes of repeat clogs in older lines.

The NMB Water report notes that lead can come from household plumbing, not the city main, so older plumbing deserves a look when you have it open anyway. Our two nearest service areas are North Miami and Aventura.

Drains are built to the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), with vents and cleanouts so they can be reached when they clog.
