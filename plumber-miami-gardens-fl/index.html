When the rainy season arrives

South Florida's summer rains fall hard, and a city of mostly single-family lots on level ground feels them. Water runs toward drains, yard lines and the low spots around houses, and a sewer or drain that is already marginal backs up when the system is full.

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, and storm-season calls often come in the evening. If a floor drain or tub is rising after heavy rain, stop using water and call. See sewer line cleaning for how we clear a main line.

A large city of single-family homes

Miami Gardens sits in northern Miami-Dade County at an elevation around ten feet, with the Broward County line to the north. It was incorporated in 2003, and growth along I-95 from the late 1960s brought many of the houses now standing.

Those houses are often 50 years old or more. That is old enough for water heaters to have been replaced several times and for original drain lines to need attention. We recommend a camera look before a major repair, so you pay for what is wrong.

Before we arrive: burst pipe or active leak at a Miami Gardens house

A house on a large lot may have its main valve far from the street, so know where it is before the day you need it.

  • Turn off the main water valve at the meter box in the yard or at the house wall.
  • Switch off breakers for any room with standing water.
  • Move furniture and electronics out of the way and use towels to slow spreading.
  • Call the 24/7 line and tell us the nearest cross street and where the water is coming from.

In a rain event, also keep the yard and the cleanout area clear so we can reach the line.

Know your shutoff before the storm

On a large lot the meter box can be a long walk from the front door. Find it on a dry day, check that the main valve turns, and note where the cleanout cap sits. Both locations save minutes when something fails at night.
