A service line leak that cannot wait

The water service line runs from the utility meter to your house, and it is usually buried. When it leaks, the meter keeps moving with every tap closed, the lawn near the line stays wet or the water pressure drops. Our 24/7 line takes these calls in North Miami Beach and offers same-day appointments when available. Shut off the main valve at the house and see whether the meter still moves.

That one test separates a service line leak from an inside leak. If the meter stops with the house valve closed, the problem is inside. If it keeps moving, it is the buried line.

Why NMB Water's inventory matters to you

In 2025 the Utilities Department completed an inventory of all water service lines, both utility-owned and customer-owned, and confirmed that there are no lead service lines in the city's system. That is a published finding from NMB Water, and it means a new line here is typically about repair, not about removing lead.

Lead can still come from household plumbing and fixtures, and the report says so. If you have older fixtures or solder, ask us about testing.

Repair or replace the line

A single leak at a fitting or a short break can be repaired with a small excavation. A line with several leaks, heavy corrosion or low flow is a candidate for replacement, which may be done in a trench or with trenchless methods depending on access.

The meter itself belongs to the utility, so ask NMB Water or your own utility before touching the meter box. We repair on the customer side and coordinate when work involves the utility's part.

Low water pressure

Low pressure at every fixture can come from a leaking service line, a partly closed valve, a failed regulator or a clogged inlet screen. We test pressure at an outside hose bib and compare it with what the inside fixtures show.

If the drop is only at one tap, the faucet may be the cause. See faucet repair. If pressure is low everywhere, the line deserves a check, and leak detection is the first step.

Reading your meter yourself

Most meters have a small flow indicator, often a triangle or a dial, that spins when any water moves. Turn off everything, close the house valve and watch the indicator for a minute.

If it moves with the house valve closed, the leak is on the buried line. Take a photo and share it with us. If NMB Water or your own utility serves the address, it can also share usage history that shows when the leak began.

Call 811 before the trench

Water service lines run underground, often near gas, electric and communication lines. Florida's one-call law requires notice before digging, and Sunshine 811 asks for at least two full business days. You can reach it by calling 811. Repairs follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the IPC. The line between the meter and the house is your side of the system, and the meter belongs to the utility.

Permits and utility coordination

Work in the right of way or on the utility side requires the utility's approval. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of North Miami Beach building department and your water utility. We tell you what we need in order to proceed.
