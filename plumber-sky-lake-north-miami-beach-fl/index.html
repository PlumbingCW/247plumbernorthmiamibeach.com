A neighborhood of larger single-family homes

A local planning summary places Sky Lake north of Miami Gardens Drive, between NE 18th and NE 22nd Avenues, and describes it as mostly relatively large single-family homes with a small commercial edge. It uses ZIP code 33179. One neighborhood guide says it was developed in the 1950s and 1960s, and that a Sky Lake Homeowners Association exists. We could not confirm either point from a government source, so treat them as guide information.

Real estate listings describe lakefront homes with private docks, and show addresses on NE 186th Drive, NE 191st Drive and NE 20th Court. Nearby neighborhoods include Sunray East, Sunray West and Highland Lakes.

Bigger homes, longer runs, more fixtures

A larger house usually has more bathrooms, more fixtures and longer pipe runs. That tends to affect hot water first. A tank at one end of a long run can leave the far bathroom waiting a long time for hot water, and that is a reason to ask about water heater installation options or a second unit.

More fixtures also means more places for a small leak to hide. If a bill rises without explanation, a leak detection visit is cheaper than opening walls to guess.

Services homeowners ask about in Sky Lake

These are the jobs that suit larger homes with yards and pools.

  • A water heater replacement sized for the number of bathrooms.
  • A tankless water heater where gas and venting allow it.
  • A water softener or filtration system when you want to change the water at the tap.
  • A water service line repair when the line from the meter to the house fails.

Yards with irrigation and pools add outdoor plumbing that can leak without being visible from the house. We can check the supply side, while pool equipment and irrigation controllers are separate trades.

Before we arrive: if you have a leak in your Sky Lake home

In a larger house, the shutoff may be far from the leak, so find it before you need it.

  • Close the fixture shutoff or the main valve at the meter.
  • Switch off power at the breaker for the wet area, and for pool equipment if water is near it.
  • Move furniture and rugs and set out towels.
  • Call and tell us the street, the room and what you see.

If you are not sure where the main valve is, say so and we will walk you through it.

Lakefront and irrigation notes

If you live on the lake, wet ground near the shore is not always a pipe leak. Test the meter before you dig. A moving meter with all fixtures off means a leak somewhere on your side.

Check your bill for the utility that serves you. NMB Water publishes an annual report on its water sources and treatment, and it is the best place to look if you have questions about the water itself.

Repairs and replacements here follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and we say which jobs need a permit when we quote.
