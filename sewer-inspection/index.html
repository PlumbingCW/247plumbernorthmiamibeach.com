What a sewer camera inspection shows

A sewer camera inspection in North Miami Beach puts a waterproof camera on a flexible cable into your drain line and records what it finds. You see the pipe wall, the joints, any roots, any standing water and the point where the line meets the street. We use it to find the cause of a repeat clog, to check a line before a repair and to inspect a home before a purchase closes.

Callers often ask whether we can actually see something in a line that slow. The answer is yes, within the limits of the pipe, and we explain those limits on the call.

What the camera can find

  • Roots entering at a joint or through a hairline crack.
  • A belly or sag where water sits and solids settle.
  • Offset joints, where two pieces of pipe no longer line up.
  • Scale or grease that has narrowed the opening.
  • A collapsed or crushed section.
  • Where the line changes material, such as cast iron to plastic.

Locating the problem, not just seeing it

The camera head carries a transmitter, so a locator at the surface can trace the pipe and mark the depth and position of anything the camera finds. That means a repair crew can open the ground at one spot instead of several.

We note the distance from the access point to each finding, so the video and the marks on your yard tell the same story. This is the information a repair quote should be built on, and it is why we recommend a look before any dig. See sewer line repair for what happens next.

Inspecting before you buy

A general home inspection does not normally put a camera in the sewer line. If you are buying a house in North Miami Beach, a camera inspection before closing tells you if the lateral has roots, offsets or a sag that could become your repair bill. Ask for the video and the written notes, and bring both to your negotiation.

The city's own history matters here. NMB Water's 2025 report marks a centennial for the city, so a house can sit on a lateral far older than the house itself.

What an inspection record is good for

A camera recording is a record of the pipe on the day it was filmed. Keep it with your house papers. It helps when you compare repair quotes, talk to an insurer or sell. The FBC Plumbing and the IPC set rules for how new work must be done, and permit rules depend on the job and the municipality, so ask the building department that covers your address what applies.

How an inspection goes

  1. Access

    We use an outdoor cleanout if there is one. If not, a toilet may need to be pulled.

  2. Clear light debris

    A very dirty line may need cabling first so the camera can see.

  3. Record the run

    The camera goes down the pipe while the monitor records and the locator marks findings.

  4. Review together

    We show you the footage, explain what matters and say what we would do about it.

What affects the price of a sewer camera inspection

An inspection is a planned diagnostic, so pricing is simpler than a repair, but a few things change it.

Length of line
A short run to the street is quicker than a long lateral.
Access
A cleanout is fast. Pulling a toilet adds time.
Cleaning before the camera
A heavily blocked line has to be opened first.
Locating and marking
Tracing and flagging findings at the surface adds a locator step.
Report and video
A written summary with the recording is part of what you receive.
