A canal-front neighborhood with one main entrance

Eastern Shores is a neighborhood of North Miami Beach with ZIP code 33160. According to the city-based descriptions collected on Wikipedia, it is bounded by Sunny Isles Boulevard on the south, Maule Lake on the west and the Intracoastal Waterway on the east, with NE 35th Avenue (also called Eastern Shores Boulevard) as the main street. Miami-Dade County planning history says bayfront development here began in 1957 on the North Graves Tract, with about 700 homes on finger canals.

Most of the neighborhood is gated, and the east side has many condominiums and townhouses. Real estate guides also describe single-family homes with deep-water access near NE 35th Avenue. Because housing types are mixed, the first thing we ask on a call is whether you own a house, a townhouse or a condo unit. That tells us who controls the main shutoff and who has to be told we are coming.

What living on the water changes about plumbing

Canal-front homes sit close to sea level, so water in the yard or under a slab is not always a plumbing leak. A leak detection visit helps separate a supply line leak from groundwater or rain. If your water bill jumped with no change in habits, that is the first thing worth checking.

Salt air is hard on metal. Exposed hose bibs, outdoor shutoff valves and unit-mounted water heater parts near the water can corrode sooner than the same parts inland. That is general plumbing knowledge and not a claim about any single street. If a valve is stiff or crusted, replace it before you need it in a hurry.

Services we are called for in Eastern Shores

Homes and units here use the same core plumbing as anywhere else in Miami-Dade County. These are the jobs that come up most often on pages like this one.

  • Slow or backed-up drains, handled with drain cleaning or hydro jetting when the line needs more than a snake.
  • Older or failing tanks, covered under water heater repair and water heater replacement.
  • Toilets that run or will not flush, on our toilet repair page.
  • Supply leaks and low pressure, which may call for burst pipe repair.

In a condo, the building decides which pipes belong to you and which belong to the association. Ask your management office before work starts, since some buildings require notice, a schedule or proof of insurance for outside plumbers.

Before we arrive: if you have a leak in an Eastern Shores home or condo

A few quick steps limit the damage while a plumber is on the way.

  • Find the shutoff for the fixture or the main shutoff for the unit or house and close it.
  • In a condo, tell your neighbors below you if water is spreading, and call the building office.
  • Turn off power at the breaker for any wet room and move rugs and electronics.
  • Call and tell us the street, the gate and where the water is coming from.

If the shutoff will not turn, say so on the call. It changes what we bring.

Gate access and which utility bills you

Tell us the gate or entrance you will use and who can open it. A guard shack at the entrance means we can lose time if the gate does not know to expect us. If you can leave your name on the visitor list, do it before we arrive.

To see which utility serves your address, check your water bill. The city of North Miami Beach runs NMB Water, and its Consumer Confidence Report describes its water sources. A bill from another provider means a different utility handles your meter.

Repairs and replacements here follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and we say which jobs need a permit when we quote.
