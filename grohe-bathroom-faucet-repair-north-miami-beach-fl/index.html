Grohe Bathroom Faucet Repair in North Miami Beach, FL

Most faucet drips come from a worn cartridge or a clogged aerator. Here is how to tell which and what a repair involves.

Call (305) 850-6382

By the North Miami Beach 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 12, 2026

Why a faucet drips or turns hard

A modern faucet has a few parts that wear. A cartridge or valve controls the flow and the mix of hot and cold. Seals and O-rings keep water inside the body. An aerator at the spout mixes air into the stream and screens out debris. When one of these wears or clogs, you get drips, a weak stream or a handle that turns stiffly.

Grohe faucets use a range of cartridges, and the right part depends on the model. Before you buy anything, identify the exact faucet, because a cartridge that looks right can still be wrong.

Start with the simple checks

  • Unscrew the aerator and rinse out any grit. A weak stream is often just a clogged screen.
  • Look for white crust around the spout, a sign of mineral build-up.
  • Check if the drip is from the spout, the base or the handle. Each points to a different part.
  • Turn off the supply stops under the sink before taking anything apart.

If a simple clean helps, you are done. If not, the cartridge is the next suspect.

How a cartridge replacement goes

We shut off the water, remove the handle and trim, loosen the retaining nut and pull the old cartridge. The new one is seated in the same orientation, sealed with fresh O-rings and tightened to the maker's guidance.

Then we flush the faucet before the aerator goes back on, so debris does not clog it right away. For other fixtures, see faucet repair.

When replacing the faucet is smarter

If the body is corroded, the finish is flaking or the faucet leaks from a crack, a new unit costs less over time than repeated repairs. If parts for an older model are no longer sold, replacement is the practical answer.

We install and test new faucets and replace worn shutoff valves at the same visit when needed.

Step by step for a typical single handle faucet

  1. Shut off the water

    Close both stops under the sink and open the faucet to release pressure.

  2. Remove the handle

    Pop off the cap, loosen the set screw and lift the handle away.

  3. Pull the cartridge

    Remove the retaining nut or clip and pull the cartridge straight out.

  4. Install the new one

    Match the old part, lubricate the O-rings with plumber's grease and reassemble.

Steps differ by model, so use the maker's guide for yours. If anything feels stuck, stop and call.

Keeping a faucet working longer

Wipe spouts and handles dry, clean the aerator every few months and avoid harsh abrasive cleaners that can strip the finish. Close the handle gently rather than cranking it shut.

If the supply stops under the sink are old or stiff, replace them while the faucet is apart. A stop that will not close is a problem on the day you need it.

When the leak is not the faucet

Water under the sink may come from the supply stops, the supply hoses or the trap, not the faucet. Dry everything with a towel and watch which joint beads up first.

A drip from a supply connector may need a new hose, and a drip from the trap may need a new washer or nut. We check all of them on a visit so you do not replace a cartridge for a leak that was somewhere else.

Cleaning and finish care

Use a soft cloth and mild soap. Harsh cleaners strip the coating and leave a dull spot, and once a finish is damaged it will not recover. If you see flaking, the faucet body may be corroding underneath.

Rinse the aerator in a bowl of vinegar and water if it has crust, then brush it gently and rinse it clean.

A local note for North Miami Beach homes

White crust on a faucet means minerals from the water. NMB Water has not published a hardness figure in its 2025 report, so test your tap or ask the utility if you want to know how much. Crust at the aerator is a sign worth acting on, because it shortens the life of cartridges.

The report also says lead can come from fixtures, so older fixtures deserve a look when you replace them. Our two nearest service areas are North Miami and Aventura.

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), sets requirements for faucets installed in new work, including how they are connected to supply.
