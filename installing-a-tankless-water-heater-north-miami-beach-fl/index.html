Installing a Tankless Water Heater in North Miami Beach, FL

A tankless heater heats water as you use it, but the install is bigger than a swap. Here is what to plan for and what to ask.

Call (305) 850-6382

By the North Miami Beach 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 12, 2026

A bigger job than swapping a tank

A tankless heater has no storage tank. When a hot tap opens, a flow sensor starts the burner or the elements and the water is heated as it passes through. The result is hot water that does not run out while the unit can keep up, and a smaller unit that mounts on a wall.

That sounds simple, but the installation is more involved than a tank swap. Gas models need a gas line of the right size and proper venting. Electric models need a lot of power. Both need water connections with isolation valves so the unit can be flushed.

Sizing it right

A tankless heater is sized by flow and temperature rise. Flow is how many gallons per minute your fixtures can draw at once. Temperature rise is the difference between the incoming water and the temperature you want.

In a warm climate the incoming water is warmer than in the north, so the rise is smaller and a given unit can serve more fixtures. Even so, a large household with two showers and a dishwasher running at the same time may need a larger unit or two. We add up your fixtures rather than guess.

What the install steps include

  1. Remove the old heater

    Shut off water and gas or power, drain and disconnect the old unit.

  2. Mount and vent

    Mount on the wall at the correct height and run approved venting for gas models.

  3. Connect water and fuel

    Install isolation valves and a pressure relief device, then gas or electric connections.

  4. Test and commission

    Check for leaks, test combustion or power draw, and set the temperature.

Planned installs are covered on our tankless water heater page.

Maintenance you will need

A tankless unit needs flushing to clear mineral scale from the heat exchanger. The isolation valves we install make that simple. Skipping flushes lets scale build up and can lead to error codes, reduced flow and early failure.

Check your manual for the schedule and ask us about a flush when you book. Our water softener and filtration page explains when treatment helps.

What a tankless install costs in time and disruption

Expect the water to be off for part of a day, and gas or power off for a while longer. If a gas line must be enlarged or a panel upgraded, those jobs may need separate scheduling or inspection, and we tell you the steps before we start.

A tankless heater also changes how you use hot water. There is a short delay before heat arrives at a far tap, and some units need a small circulation setup to reduce it. Ask about it if your bathrooms are far from the heater.

When a tank is still the better choice

A tankless unit is not always the best answer. If your gas line is small, your panel is full or your household uses hot water in short bursts, a modern tank can serve well and cost less to install.

If your current heater just failed, the fastest way back to hot water is often a tank. You can plan a tankless upgrade later. See water heater replacement for the quick path.

Warranty and records

Keep the receipt, the serial number and the install date. Most tankless warranties require proper installation by a licensed professional and regular maintenance, and the maker may ask for proof.

We leave you with the paperwork and a written note of the settings so you can show it later.

A local note for North Miami Beach homes

NMB Water has not published a hardness figure in its 2025 report, so test your tap or ask the utility before you decide on scale protection. Hardness is what makes a tankless flush more important, and an actual reading beats a guess.

The city says its treatment includes lime softening and nanofiltration or reverse osmosis, and the supply is blended before delivery. Venting and gas rules vary by building, so ask the building department what applies before installation. Our two nearest service areas are North Miami and Aventura.

Venting, gas piping and the relief valve are covered by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and by the maker's installation manual.
