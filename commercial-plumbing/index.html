Commercial plumbing emergencies at any hour

A plumbing failure in a business costs revenue every hour it stays open. Our commercial plumbing service in North Miami Beach answers the 24/7 line and offers same-day appointments when available. If water is reaching floors or merchandise, shut off the building main or the local valve, switch off power to wet areas and call.

Leak detection for a business

Commercial leak detection is harder than residential because building systems are larger. We isolate zones, check risers and use acoustic and thermal tools. We write up findings for the owner or manager, and we are honest about limits: not every leak can be located without opening a space.

See leak detection for the methods.

Grease, backflow and storm drains

Grease traps and interceptors need regular cleaning, and a full trap sends grease into the line. Backflow preventers protect the public water supply and may require periodic testing under local rules, so ask the City of North Miami Beach or your utility what applies to your property.

Storm drains and exterior catch basins clog with leaves and silt during heavy rain. We clear them and flag drainage problems. For lines that keep clogging, see hydro jetting.

Restaurants and food service

A restaurant kitchen puts more strain on plumbing than almost any other business. Dish machines, prep sinks, floor drains and a grease interceptor all share lines that carry hot water, soap and fat, and a failure during service means lost covers.

We look at how the kitchen drains are laid out, where the grease goes and whether the floor drains share a line with the dining room restrooms. A kitchen that keeps backing up in the same place usually needs a camera look and a scheduled jetting plan, not another cable run.

Shops, offices and medical suites

Retail and office tenants usually deal with restroom stoppages, slow floor drains, leaks from a unit above and water heaters that serve a break room. Medical and dental suites add sinks, vacuum and suction lines and sterilization equipment that have their own plumbing needs.

If you lease space, the landlord may be responsible for the building lines and you for the fixtures inside your suite. We document where a problem sits so the right party is called, which saves time and arguments.

What we ask when you call about a building

  • The address, the suite and whether the building is open now.
  • Which fixtures or areas are affected and whether water is spreading.
  • Whether you can reach the main shutoff and the cleanout.
  • Who can give access and who approves the work.
  • Whether the property has a grease trap or backflow device.

Those five answers let us send the right equipment and a plumber who can start without waiting on a second visit.

What to do before we arrive at your business

  • Tell staff and customers to stay clear of wet floors.
  • Close the main shutoff or the fixture stop if you can reach it safely.
  • Move stock and electronics away from the water.
  • Note the time the problem started for your records.

Commercial rules to plan around

Businesses face rules that homes do not. The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the IPC, covers fixtures counts, grease interceptors and backflow protection. Food service has added rules from the health department. Ask the building department and the health department which apply to your space.

Landlords, property managers and condo associations

If you manage property, tell us the unit, the access contact and any building rules. We coordinate with tenants, document the work and give you a clear invoice. Backups in a multi-unit building often start in a shared stack, so we check the building line first.

For main-line issues, see sewer line cleaning.
