No hot water emergency: when the tank has failed

When a tank leaks through its body, replacement is the only fix, and in a home with a slab floor the water spreads fast. Our water heater replacement crews work in North Miami Beach around the clock, and same-day appointments are offered when available. Turn off the cold water supply to the heater, shut off power or gas, and keep towels at the base until we arrive.

The replacement itself is a planned job once the old tank is drained. We match the size and fuel to how your household uses hot water and make sure the new unit is set up properly for the room it lives in.

What the replacement includes

  • Drain and disconnect the old unit.
  • Install the new heater with new flex connectors and a relief valve discharge line.
  • Set a drain pan and a drain route where the space calls for it.
  • Test the thermostat, the burner or elements and the relief valve.
  • Haul the old tank away.

The details change with the unit and the closet, and we describe them in the quote.

Condos and townhomes

In a condo, the water heater often sits in a closet near the unit entry, and a leak can run down to the neighbor below. Many buildings have rules about who can enter, when work is allowed and what permits apply.

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of North Miami Beach building department and your association before the work. We can tell you what we need from the manager and help with the paperwork on our side.

What to do before we arrive to replace a heater

  • Shut off the cold supply and the power or gas.
  • Clear a path from the heater to the door.
  • Take a photo of the data label on the old tank.

Permits and code on a replacement

A heater swap often needs a permit. The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, built on the IPC, sets requirements for the relief valve, venting, expansion control and gas or electrical connections. Permit rules depend on the job and the municipality, so ask the building department that covers your address what applies. We pull permits where they are required and tell you if one applies.

Why the old unit failed matters

Tanks fail from the inside. Sediment settles, the liner wears and the anode rod is used up. NMB Water's 2025 report notes that its treatment blends lime-softened, nanofiltered and reverse osmosis water and adds chloramine for disinfection, and it recommends flushing a heater at least twice a year if water looks discolored.

We look at what failed and tell you how to extend the life of the next one. If the old unit leaked from a fitting rather than the tank, see water heater repair before committing to a swap.
